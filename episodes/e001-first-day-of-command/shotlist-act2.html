Style: Ultra-photorealistic cinematic mockumentary — The Office (US) documentary grammar set in July 1775. Shot on 35mm film, handheld documentary camera. Fully photographic realism, never animated.
World: Continental Army camp, Cambridge, Massachusetts, summer 1775. Everything period-authentic — homespun linen, mud, canvas tents, muskets, goats. Absolutely no modern objects, no anachronisms.

Continuity: Characters, wardrobe, props, and environment identical across every cut — match the attached reference images exactly. No identity drift. No props appearing in hands or mouths that are not scripted: no pipes, no toothpicks, no cigarettes, no papers.
Physics: Real weight and inertia, correct contact shadows, no floating props.
Technical: 24fps, 180° shutter motion blur, maximum photoreal detail, no jitter, no slow motion — all movement at natural real-time speed.

Characters:
GENERAL WASHINGTON — matches the first reference image exactly: mid-40s, tall, broad, auburn hair tied back in a queue, immaculate blue-and-buff Continental general's uniform, white gloves held in his left hand. Two days into command; his patience has thinned but his posture has not.
SERGEANT — New England militiaman, late 30s, sunburnt, patchy brown wool coat with mismatched buttons, three-day beard, the relaxed confidence of an elected official.
EZEKIEL — lanky private, early 20s, homespun linen shirt, hat tipped over his eyes, spectacularly at ease.

Scene:
Mid-morning, overcast; the muddy camp work area matches the second reference image. A half-dug trench with two shovels standing in it, untouched. Ezekiel lounges against a split-rail fence at frame right, ten feet from the trench. Washington stands frame-left facing the Sergeant frame-right, eight feet apart, the trench between them. Camp bustle continues behind them, indifferent.

CUT 1 — Handheld 35mm two-shot, chest height, slow lateral drift right, Washington frame-left, Sergeant frame-right:
Washington's eyes travel from the empty trench to the lounging private — an unhurried, complete assessment — then back to the Sergeant. A measured beat. Evenly, quietly: "Sergeant. Order that man to dig." His chin indicates Ezekiel with the smallest possible movement. The Sergeant follows the gesture, then looks back, already apologetic.

CUT 2 — Snap-zoom past their shoulders to Ezekiel at the fence, long-lens 85mm feel, quick refocus:
Ezekiel, eyes closed under his tipped hat, arms folded, one boot crossed over the other, profoundly at peace. He exhales contentedly. Hold the comic beat a full two seconds. A goat bleats somewhere off-screen. Nobody speaks.

CUT 3 — Reverse to the Sergeant, 50mm medium, static, Washington's shoulder soft in the left foreground:
The Sergeant leans in half a step, confidential, with the warm, patient tone of a man explaining something obvious to a foreigner: "Can't, sir. Ezekiel voted for me." A small, genuinely sympathetic shrug.

CUT 4 — 85mm close on Washington, static, shallow focus, overcast key from his right:
A micro-pause. His jaw sets a fraction. Flat, without heat: "That is not how armies work." From off-screen, the Sergeant's voice, cheerfully agreeing: "It's how this one works." Washington blinks once, and his eyes travel slowly — deliberately — to the documentary lens and hold there as his chest rises with one long, controlled breath.
