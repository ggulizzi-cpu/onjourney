# HIS EXCELLENCY — Episode 1: "First Day of Command" (v2)

**Runtime:** ~4:55 · 30 blocks · Cambridge, Massachusetts, July 1775
**Logline:** George Washington arrives to take command of the Continental Army and
discovers that the army is fourteen thousand men, nine bullets each, and a strongly
worded cause.

Every block is one Seedance 2.0 generation. **No static shots:** each block carries a
CAMERA line with movement and, where the scene calls for it, internal cuts (Seedance
multi-shot prompting). Confessionals stay near-locked-off with documentary micro-drift
and slow push-ins — that stillness is the joke's frame, not laziness.

---

## COLD OPEN (~36s)

**1.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Low aerial drift over the camp toward golden-hour sun → internal cut: lateral
track past tents and goats → whip-pan to the cannon as it fires; handheld shake on the
blast, chickens scatter through frame.
Sprawling militia camp, laundry drying on stacked muskets. Caption: **CAMBRIDGE,
MASSACHUSETTS — JULY 1775.** A field cannon, a MILITIAMAN leaning on it with a pipe.
The cannon FIRES by itself, shredding an empty tent.

> **MILITIAMAN** *(calling out, unbothered)*: That one's loaded, then.

**2.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Rising crane behind the riders as they crest the hill, camp revealed below →
internal cut: front two-shot, slow dolly-in as the silence lands.
GEORGE WASHINGTON on horseback — immaculate blue and buff, the most put-together object
in Massachusetts. JOSEPH REED beside him.

> **GEORGE:** Mr. Reed. Where is the army?
> **REED:** …This is the army, General.

**3.** *(8s)*
**CAMERA:** Slow snap-zoom from medium to tight close-up — the mockumentary punch-in.
George's perfect stoic face. One nostril twitches.

> **GEORGE:** Splendid.

**4.** *(8s — TITLE CARD)*
**CAMERA:** Candle-lit parchment, slow push; engraved lettering catches the light.
**HIS EXCELLENCY.** Fife-and-drum sting.

## ACT ONE — THE HANDOVER (~88s)

**5.** *(10s — CONFESSIONAL: GEORGE)*
**CAMERA:** Locked-off with handheld micro-drift; slow push-in that lands exactly on the
look to lens.
Vassall House study, window light. Posture of a marble statue.

> **GEORGE:** I did not seek this command. I attended Congress for four weeks…
> *(beat)* …in full uniform. *(to lens)* No one else thought to.

**6.** *(12s)*
**CAMERA:** Handheld arc around the two men under the elm → internal cut: over-the-
shoulder on the ledger handoff → quick pan following Ward as he simply walks away.
Handover ceremony, sparse crowd, one goat in attendance. ARTEMAS WARD — gray, unwell,
visibly done — hands George a single thin ledger.

> **WARD:** It's a siege. They're in there. We're out here. Try to keep it that way.
> **GEORGE:** General Ward, your service—
> **WARD:** Wonderful. I'm going to go lie down.

**7.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Long-lens documentary snoop from behind tents, refocus pull between the two
men — the crew is eavesdropping.
Two MILITIAMEN watch from the crowd, arms crossed.

> **MILITIAMAN 1:** Who's the tall fellow?
> **MILITIAMAN 2:** New management. Virginia.
> **MILITIAMAN 1** *(spits)*: A *southerner*? Giving orders to *us*?

**8.** *(10s — CONFESSIONAL: REED)*
**CAMERA:** Locked-off, micro-drift; tiny reframe on the beat, as if the operator laughed.
Young, wry, direct to lens.

> **REED:** General Washington believes in discipline, order, and the dignity of
> command. *(beat)* He has been here one hour.

**9.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Slow lateral dolly across the officers' faces mid-speech — a police lineup
of slouching — → internal cut: snap-zoom to the tankard as it rises.
George addresses his assembled OFFICERS. Half slouch. One is barefoot. One holds a
tankard. It is ten in the morning.

> **GEORGE:** Gentlemen. Together we shall forge these men into one army of the United
> Colonies.
> **TANKARD OFFICER** *(raising it)*: Huzzah!
> **THE REST** *(ragged, off-time)*: huzzah.

**10.** *(8s)*
**CAMERA:** Tight two-shot, handheld; the frame tilts down just enough to catch George's
knuckles whiten on his gloves.
George holds a diplomatic smile that does not reach his eyes.

> **GEORGE** *(quiet, to Reed)*: Take a letter.

**11.** *(10s — THE LETTERS GAG)*
**CAMERA:** Close on quill scratching by candlelight, slow push → hard internal cut:
daylight, George bowing graciously to those same officers → cut back to the quill
underlining a word.
Night. George writes; his voiceover is courteous murder.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "Dear brother. I have arrived safely. The officers here are…"
> *(on screen: the gracious bow)* "…the most indifferent kind of men I ever saw."

## ACT TWO — THE INSPECTION (~102s)

**12.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Handheld walk-and-talk tracking backward in front of the tour → internal
cut: George's POV tilt down into the trench, instant tilt back up.
George presses a handkerchief to his face. Reed looks at the camera.

> **GEORGE:** What is that trench?
> **SOLDIER** *(proud)*: The kitchen, sir.
> **GEORGE:** It is adjacent to the latrine.
> **SOLDIER:** *(pause)* "Adjacent" is a strong word.

**13.** *(12s)*
**CAMERA:** KNOX enters frame like a cannonball — whip-pan to catch him → energetic
handheld three-shot, books teetering; internal cut to George's blink.
A round, beaming young man in spectacles, arms full of books — HENRY KNOX.

> **KNOX:** General! Henry Knox — I kept a bookshop in Boston. The British are stabling
> horses in it. *Horses.* Among the *books.* Point is: I've read everything ever
> printed on artillery.
> **GEORGE:** You've… read about it.
> **KNOX:** Cover to cover. Twice!

**14.** *(8s — CONFESSIONAL: KNOX)*
**CAMERA:** Locked-off, micro-drift; he leans into the lens on the last word and goes
soft-focus for a blink before the operator corrects.
Radiant. Holds up a worn book.

> **KNOX:** Some men are born soldiers. I became one… through reading.

**15.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Static-ish two-shot that snap-zooms to the lounging private exactly when
he's mentioned, then back.
George attempts chain of command with a SERGEANT while a private lounges on a fence.

> **GEORGE:** Sergeant. Order that man to dig.
> **SERGEANT:** Can't, sir. Ezekiel voted for me.
> **GEORGE:** That is not how armies work.
> **SERGEANT:** It's how this one works.

**16.** *(12s)*
**CAMERA:** Dogs enter first — low tracking shot at dog height → tilt up to LEE mid-
stride → loose handheld three-shot; internal cut on "Some taken."
CHARLES LEE — gaunt, wild-eyed, brilliant, trailed by three dogs of descending size.

> **LEE:** Washington. Or should I say — *His Excellency.* *(to the camera crew)* I was
> in actual wars. European ones.
> **GEORGE:** General Lee. Whose dogs are these?
> **LEE:** Mine. Better company than most generals. No offense.
> **GEORGE:** Some taken.

**17.** *(8s — CONFESSIONAL: LEE)*
**CAMERA:** Locked-off; the dog on his lap stares directly into the lens the entire time.
A second dog is audible offscreen.

> **LEE:** Congress chose George because he is tall and owns a uniform. I speak five
> languages. *(the dog barks)* Six.

**18.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Wide on the rum tent crowd → snap-zoom to George on "privilege" → internal
cut: slow-motion-feeling stillness as hundreds answer in unison → George turns to lens,
camera holds, terrified to move.

> **SOLDIER:** We'll fight, General. But the rum ration's non-negotiable.
> **GEORGE:** The rum is a privilege—
> **THE ENTIRE CAMP** *(instant, unison)*: **NON-NEGOTIABLE.**

George slowly turns and looks directly into the documentary camera. First time.

**19.** *(10s — CONFESSIONAL: GEORGE)*
**CAMERA:** Locked-off; push-in begins precisely on "Yesterday."
Hands folded. Very still.

> **GEORGE:** In Virginia, when I give an instruction, it is followed. Here, it is…
> put to a vote. *(long pause)* Yesterday I lost a vote.

**20.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Tilt up from the two men to geese crossing the sky, musket smoke drifting
through frame → tilt back down onto Reed's dawning realization.
Muskets crackle. Soldiers whoop, firing at geese overhead.

> **GEORGE:** Why are they firing?
> **REED:** Geese.
> **GEORGE:** Are they hitting any?
> **REED:** No, sir. It's mostly morale.
> **GEORGE:** That is *gunpowder*.
> **REED:** …I'll ask about the powder.

## ACT THREE — NINE ROUNDS (~72s)

**21.** *(12s)*
**CAMERA:** Slow orbit around the war-room table, candle flames licking the lens flare →
internal cut: top-down insert of the single sheet sliding across the map.
HQ, dusk. HORATIO GATES — reading glasses, the energy of a man announcing a bake sale.
Around the table: Reed, Lee, Knox.

> **GATES:** General. The complete inventory of gunpowder.
> **GEORGE** *(reading)*: This says thirty-six barrels.
> **GATES:** Yes, sir. I double-checked. It got worse.
> **GEORGE:** For fourteen thousand men.
> **GATES:** Nine rounds each. Ish.

**22.** *(12s — THE SILENCE)*
**CAMERA:** One unbroken, agonizing push-in on George. No cuts. The one static-feeling
shot in the episode, and it's the loudest.
George stares at the paper. The clock ticks. Officers trade glances. Hold. Hold.

> **LEE** *(whispering, leaning into the lens)*: He's been like this for half an hour.

**23.** *(8s — CONFESSIONAL: REED)*
**CAMERA:** Locked-off, micro-drift, no push — flat delivery, flat frame.

> **REED:** The greatest army on Earth is across that water. We have nine bullets a man
> and a strongly worded cause.

**24.** *(12s)*
**CAMERA:** George rises out of frame; camera finds him again at the window in
silhouette against Boston's lights → internal cut: the officers' faces in candlelight,
one slow pan across them on "We bluff."

> **GEORGE:** Not a word of this leaves the room. If the enemy learns how weak we are,
> this war ends by Friday.
> **KNOX** *(softly)*: …So we bluff.
> **GEORGE:** We bluff.

**25.** *(10s — THE SINCERE BEAT)*
**CAMERA:** Close on the quill, then drift up to George's face lit by a single candle —
gentle, patient, no comedy in the frame.
Night. Voiceover, and for once there is no joke in his voice.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "My dearest Patsy. I have taken command of an army that has no
> powder, no order, and no intention of listening to me."

**26.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Slow floating drift through the sleeping camp — banked campfires, a drummer
boy asleep against his drum → internal cut: Knox reading an artillery manual by
firelight, mouthing the words.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "And yet — when I rode in today, not one of them was marching
> home. Perhaps that is an army after all."

**27.** *(8s)*
**CAMERA:** Insert: wax seal pressed onto the letter → tilt up to the smallest real
smile of the episode.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "Postscript. Send socks. Four thousand pairs."

## TAG (~24s)

**28.** *(10s)*
**CAMERA:** Same framing as shot 1 — the audience recognizes it instantly → cannon
fires; whole-camp flinch in a fast whip-pan → George crosses frame mid-stride without
breaking step, tracked in profile.
Morning. The cold-open cannon. Same militiaman, same pipe.

> **GEORGE** *(without turning)*: Log the powder.
> **MILITIAMAN:** Aye. That one was loaded too.

**29.** *(8s — FINAL CONFESSIONAL: GEORGE)*
**CAMERA:** Locked-off; the slowest push of the episode, landing tight on "It will have
to do."

> **GEORGE:** Do I believe we can win? *(pause)* I believe we cannot be *seen* losing.
> It is not the same thing. *(beat)* It will have to do.

**30.** *(6s — END CARD)*
**CAMERA:** Parchment end card, candlelight flicker, slow settle.
**HIS EXCELLENCY** · *written & directed by nobody who was there* · fife sting, out.

## Sound & music plan

- **Dialogue:** Seedance native on-camera audio (lip-synced). Direction embedded per
  prompt: dry, deadpan, naturalistic — *The Office* delivery, never announcer-y.
- **Voiceover (the letters):** recorded separately with an expressive TTS voice
  auditioned before the shoot (3 candidates, we pick together or I pick the least
  synthetic). Slight room tone + quill SFX under it so it sits in the world.
- **Music:** original fife-and-drum main sting (title + end card), sparse period
  underscore only where the sincere beat needs it (blocks 25–27) — comedy plays dry,
  without score, like The Office.
- **SFX:** Seedance native ambience per shot (camp noise, musket cracks, dogs), plus a
  sweetening pass in ffmpeg: cannon boom with real low end, chicken flurry, clock tick
  in the war room, unison "NON-NEGOTIABLE" doubled for crowd weight.

## Historical notes (what's real vs. compressed)

- **Real:** Washington took command at Cambridge July 3, 1775; wore his uniform
  throughout the Second Continental Congress; the camp's sanitation was notorious; New
  England militia elected their officers; the rum ration was fiercely defended; Charles
  Lee's dog pack and his resentment; Knox was a 25-year-old Boston bookseller turned
  artillerist whose shop the British trashed; Ward was unwell and soon offered his
  resignation; Washington's private letters really were that savage ("the most
  indifferent kind of people I ever saw").
- **Compressed:** the gunpowder crisis (36 barrels, ~9 rounds/man) was discovered
  August 3, 1775 — moved to week one. Washington's reported reaction — not speaking for
  half an hour (Gen. Sullivan's account) — is real and is block 22.
- **Invented, for value:** the misfiring cannon; the unison "non-negotiable"; Ward's
  exit line (spirit accurate: he wanted out). Factual density: ~90%.
