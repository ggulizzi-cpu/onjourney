# HIS EXCELLENCY — Episode 1: "First Day of Command"

**Runtime:** ~4:55 · 30 shots · Cambridge, Massachusetts, July 1775
**Logline:** George Washington arrives to take command of the Continental Army and
discovers that the army is fourteen thousand men, nine bullets each, and a strongly
worded cause.

---

## COLD OPEN (~36s)

**1.** *(10s — wide, golden hour)* The Continental camp sprawls across a Cambridge
hillside: hundreds of mismatched tents, laundry drying on stacked muskets, goats
wandering. On-screen caption: **CAMBRIDGE, MASSACHUSETTS — JULY 1775.** A field cannon
sits at frame right; a MILITIAMAN leans on it, smoking a pipe. The cannon FIRES by
itself, shredding an (empty) tent. Chickens detonate in all directions.

> **MILITIAMAN** *(calling out, unbothered)*: That one's loaded, then.

**2.** *(10s)* GEORGE WASHINGTON crests the hill on horseback — immaculate blue and
buff uniform, the single most put-together object in Massachusetts. Beside him, JOSEPH
REED. George surveys the camp. A long silence.

> **GEORGE:** Mr. Reed. Where is the army?
> **REED:** …This is the army, General.

**3.** *(8s — close on George)* Perfect stoic face. One nostril twitches.

> **GEORGE:** Splendid.

**4.** *(8s — TITLE CARD)* Parchment and engraving style, fife-and-drum sting:
**HIS EXCELLENCY.**

## ACT ONE — THE HANDOVER (~88s)

**5.** *(10s — CONFESSIONAL: GEORGE)* Seated in the Vassall House study, posture of a
marble statue.

> **GEORGE:** I did not seek this command. I merely attended Congress, for four weeks…
> *(beat)* …wearing a uniform. *(looks to camera)*

**6.** *(10s)* Handover ceremony under the great elm. Outgoing commander ARTEMAS WARD —
older, gray, visibly done — hands George a single thin ledger. Sparse crowd. A goat
attends.

> **WARD:** The siege, the men, the… *(gestures at everything)* …all of it. Yours now.
> **GEORGE:** General Ward, your service—
> **WARD:** Wonderful. I'm going to go lie down.

**7.** *(10s)* Two MILITIAMEN watch from the crowd, arms crossed.

> **MILITIAMAN 1:** Who's the tall fellow?
> **MILITIAMAN 2:** New management. Virginia.
> **MILITIAMAN 1** *(spits)*: A *southerner*? Giving orders to *us*?

**8.** *(10s — CONFESSIONAL: REED)* Young, wry, direct to lens.

> **REED:** General Washington believes in discipline, order, and the dignity of
> command. *(beat)* He has been here one hour.

**9.** *(10s)* George addresses his assembled OFFICERS outside HQ. Half of them slouch.
One is barefoot. One holds a tankard. It is ten in the morning.

> **GEORGE:** Gentlemen. Together we shall forge these men into one army of the United
> Colonies.
> **TANKARD OFFICER** *(raising it)*: Huzzah!
> **THE REST** *(ragged, off-time)*: huzzah.

**10.** *(8s)* George holds a diplomatic smile that does not reach his eyes.

> **GEORGE** *(quiet, to Reed)*: Take a letter.

**11.** *(10s — THE LETTERS GAG)* Night. George writes by candlelight; his voiceover is
courteous murder. Intercut: George that afternoon, bowing graciously to those same
officers.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "Dear brother. I have arrived safely. The officers here are…"
> *(on screen: George smiling, shaking hands)* "…the most indifferent kind of men I
> ever saw."

## ACT TWO — THE INSPECTION (~102s)

**12.** *(10s)* Camp walking tour. George presses a handkerchief to his face near a
suspicious trench. Reed looks at the camera.

> **GEORGE:** What is that trench?
> **SOLDIER** *(proud)*: The kitchen, sir.
> **GEORGE:** …And the latrine?
> **SOLDIER:** *(long pause)* We should probably pick a different trench for that.

**13.** *(12s)* A round, beaming young man in spectacles intercepts the tour, arms full
of books — HENRY KNOX.

> **KNOX:** General! Henry Knox — I kept a bookshop in Boston. Well. *Keep.* The British
> are using it as a stable. Point is: I have read everything ever printed on artillery.
> **GEORGE:** You've… read about it.
> **KNOX:** Cover to cover. Twice!

**14.** *(8s — CONFESSIONAL: KNOX)* Radiant. Holds up a worn book.

> **KNOX:** Some men are born soldiers. I became one… through reading.

**15.** *(10s)* George attempts chain of command with a SERGEANT while a private lounges
against a fence.

> **GEORGE:** Sergeant. Order that man to dig.
> **SERGEANT:** Oh — I can't order Ezekiel, sir. He voted for me.

**16.** *(12s)* CHARLES LEE arrives — gaunt, wild-eyed, brilliant, trailed by three
dogs of descending size.

> **LEE:** Washington. Or should I say — *His Excellency.* *(to the camera crew)* I was
> in actual wars. European ones.
> **GEORGE:** General Lee. Whose dogs are these?
> **LEE:** Mine. Better company than most generals. No offense.
> **GEORGE:** Some taken.

**17.** *(8s — CONFESSIONAL: LEE)* A dog occupies his lap. Another is audible offscreen.

> **LEE:** Congress chose George because he is tall and owns a uniform. I speak five
> languages. *(the dog barks)* Six.

**18.** *(10s)* George addresses a crowd of soldiers at the rum tent.

> **SOLDIER:** We'll fight, General. But the rum ration's non-negotiable.
> **GEORGE:** The rum is a privilege—
> **THE ENTIRE CAMP** *(instant, unison, hundreds of voices)*: **NON-NEGOTIABLE.**

George slowly turns and looks directly into the documentary camera. First time.

**19.** *(10s — CONFESSIONAL: GEORGE)* Hands folded. Very still.

> **GEORGE:** In Virginia, when I give an instruction, it is followed. Here, it is…
> put to a vote. *(long pause)* Yesterday I lost a vote.

**20.** *(8s)* Musket fire crackles. Soldiers whoop, shooting at geese passing overhead.

> **GEORGE:** Why are they firing?
> **REED:** Geese.
> **GEORGE:** Is powder not precious?
> **REED:** …I'll ask about the powder.

## ACT THREE — NINE ROUNDS (~72s)

**21.** *(12s)* HQ war room, dusk, candles. HORATIO GATES — reading glasses, the energy
of a man announcing a bake sale — slides a single sheet across the table to George.
Officers ring the table: Reed, Lee, Knox.

> **GATES:** General. The complete inventory of gunpowder.
> **GEORGE** *(reading)*: This says thirty-six barrels.
> **GATES:** Yes, sir.
> **GEORGE:** For fourteen thousand men.
> **GATES:** Nine rounds each. Ish.

**22.** *(12s — THE SILENCE)* George stares at the paper. Does not move. The clock
ticks. Officers trade glances. The camera pushes in slowly. Hold. Hold.

> **LEE** *(whispering, leaning to the lens)*: He's been like this for half an hour.

**23.** *(8s — CONFESSIONAL: REED)*

> **REED:** The greatest army on Earth is across that water. We have nine bullets a man
> and a strongly worded cause.

**24.** *(12s)* George rises, crosses to the window. Boston's lights glitter across the
bay. He speaks without turning.

> **GEORGE:** Not a word of this leaves the room. If the enemy learns how weak we are,
> this war ends by Friday.
> **KNOX** *(softly)*: …So we bluff.
> **GEORGE:** We bluff.

**25.** *(10s — THE SINCERE BEAT)* Night. George at his desk, writing. Voiceover, and
for once there is no joke in his voice.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "My dearest Patsy. I have taken command of an army that has no
> powder, no order, and no intention of listening to me."

**26.** *(10s)* The voiceover continues over the sleeping camp: campfires banked low; a
drummer boy asleep against his drum; Knox reading an artillery manual by firelight,
mouthing the words.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "And yet — when I rode in today, not one of them was marching
> home. Perhaps that is an army after all."

**27.** *(8s)* George seals the letter. The smallest real smile of the episode.

> **GEORGE (V.O.):** "Postscript. Send socks. Four thousand pairs."

## TAG (~24s)

**28.** *(10s)* Morning. The cold-open cannon. The same militiaman leaning on it, same
pipe. It FIRES by itself again. The whole camp flinches — except George, mid-stride
through frame, who does not break step.

> **GEORGE** *(without turning)*: Log the powder.
> **MILITIAMAN:** Aye. That one was loaded too.

**29.** *(8s — FINAL CONFESSIONAL: GEORGE)*

> **GEORGE:** Do I believe we can win? *(pause)* I believe we cannot be *seen* losing.
> It is not the same thing. *(beat)* It will have to do.

**30.** *(6s — END CARD)* Parchment: **HIS EXCELLENCY** · *written & directed by
nobody who was there* · fife sting, out.

## Historical notes (what's real vs. compressed)

- **Real:** Washington took command at Cambridge July 3, 1775; wore his uniform
  throughout the Second Continental Congress; the camp's sanitation was notorious; New
  England militia elected their officers; the rum ration was fiercely defended; Charles
  Lee's dog pack and his resentment; Knox was a 25-year-old Boston bookseller turned
  artillerist; Ward was unwell and soon offered his resignation; Washington's private
  letters really were that savage ("the most indifferent kind of people I ever saw").
- **Compressed:** the gunpowder crisis (36 barrels, ~9 rounds/man) was discovered
  August 3, 1775 — moved to week one. Washington's reported reaction — not speaking
  for half an hour (Gen. Sullivan's account) — is real and is shot 22.
- **Invented for comedy tolerance:** the misfiring cannon; the unison "non-negotiable";
  Ward's exit line (spirit accurate: he wanted out).
